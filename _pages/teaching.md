Most of my research is based on fieldwork data. You can read about some of the languages and projects I'm working on below.

<h1>Baatonum</h1>

Baatonum (ISO: bba) is a Niger-Congo language spoken in Benin. My work on Yemba began in a fields methods class taught at Rutgers in Spring 2023, and I have continued to work on the language since then. Currently, I am working with Mark Baker and Thuong (Beryl) Bui on clitics, with Maria Kouneli and Thuong (Beryl) Bui on causatives, and with Jiayuan Chen on definites.


<h1>Kazakh</h1>

Kazakh (ISO: kaz) is a Turkic language primarily spoken in Kazakhstan. In the summer of 2025, I went to Kazakhstan with some other Rutgers students for a fieldwork trip led by <a href="https://www.adammccollum.net/about">Adam McCollum</a>. My current interest in Kazakh involves <i>say</i>-complementation. Kazakh embedded clauses are often introduced by <i>dep</i>, which is a converbial form of the verb <i>deu</i> "to say". Interestingly, while <i>dep</i> has a converb use, the <i>dep</i> that introduces embedded clauses can also behave like a true complementizer. With certain predicates related to speech, however, <i>dep</i> still behaves like a verb rather than a complementizer. You can check out our LSA proceedings paper on <i>dep</i> <a href="https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/PLSA/article/view/6083/5801">here</a>.
